Animation Spotlight: Falling Leaves

An advanced ki blade art, made as a cinematic and as a game skill from one Blender animation, using the Wandering Crescent pipeline.

By Fotoh Mbaku · 2 min read

Animation

  • Built from the game's motion-capture clips: an axe standing jump for the gather, a backflip for the leap, an axe horizontal attack for the coil and sweep, and a ninja landing for the fall and guard.
  • Cape, sash, skirt and hair baked as verlet ropes in this sequence's wind.

Sequence

  • Frames 0–31: crouch while ki rises from the ground.
  • 31–56: backflip to about 7.5 m, leaving a crater.
  • 56–84: hold at the apex, blade drawn back. The blade turns black.
  • 84–101: forward plunge. A horizontal sweep releases one large crescent wave.
  • 101–110: six smaller waves split off and hit the ground, each leaving a crater.
  • 110–252: slow drift down, landing in a ready guard.

Rendering

  • Same ruined courtyard as Wandering Crescent, turned to autumn, with a new black material for the blade.
  • Ten shots, with every effect rebuilt per frame by script.
  • The soundtrack is synthesised by a Python script.

In game

  • The skill fallingLeaves is the Test Book's third skill, with five beats.
  • The Blender animation is exported as an in-place clip in the game skeleton's bone space, with every key exact.
  • The waves reuse the Aura Wave model. A new Blender script builds a small ki leaf for the leftover particles.
  • New beat fields cover falling, drift, a capped fall speed for the float, the black blade, the crater and wave release, and a camera that pulls back to keep the full height in frame.
  • Covered by an automated test in the Godot suite.
